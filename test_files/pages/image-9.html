Canada Goose
The Canada goose is a large wild goose native to North America and is one of the continent’s most familiar waterfowl species. It is recognized by its long black neck, black head, and distinctive white cheek patch, along with a brown body and broad wings. Despite being closely associated with lakes, ponds, and wetlands, Canada geese also spend plenty of time grazing on grasses in fields, parks, and open green spaces.
Highly adaptable and often seen in flocks, Canada geese feed mainly on grasses, sedges, and aquatic vegetation, though they may also eat grains and berries when available. Many populations migrate seasonally in well-known V-shaped formations, while others remain year-round in areas with reliable food and open water. Their loud, honking calls and strong pair bonds have made them one of the most recognizable birds across much of their range.
