Jellyfish in San Francisco Aquarium
A drifting cluster of Jellyfish fills the frame in this underwater scene, their translucent bodies suspended gracefully in the water. About a dozen individuals can be seen at varying depths and orientations, their bell-shaped forms gently pulsing as they move. Soft lighting filters through the water, illuminating their delicate structures and trailing tentacles, while the surrounding space fades into a calm, aquatic backdrop. Jellyfish are among the oldest known groups of animals, with fossil evidence suggesting they have existed for over 500 million years. They move primarily by contracting their bell to propel themselves, though ocean currents often carry them over long distances. This image, captured at Aquarium of the Bay, highlights the serene yet dynamic nature of these marine invertebrates, offering a glimpse into the quiet beauty of life beneath the surface.
