Trees with branches covered in hoar-frost
Hoar frost forms when water vapor in the air freezes directly onto cold surfaces, creating delicate white ice crystals that can coat twigs, branches, and other exposed objects. Unlike ordinary frozen dew, it develops through deposition rather than from liquid water first collecting and then freezing, and it is most likely to appear in calm, humid conditions when temperatures remain below freezing. In many temperate regions, leafless trees are a familiar part of winter. Deciduous species shed their leaves in autumn as part of a seasonal cycle that helps conserve water and energy during colder months. When frost settles across bare branches, it can briefly transform an ordinary woodland scene into a striking display of pattern, texture, and winter light.
