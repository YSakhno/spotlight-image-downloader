Moon in the night sky
The Moon is Earth’s only natural satellite and the brightest object in the night sky after the Sun. Formed about 4.5 billion years ago, it orbits our planet at an average distance of roughly 384,400 kilometers (238,900 miles). Its familiar phases—from new moon to full moon—result from changing angles between the Sun, Earth, and the Moon as it travels around Earth. The Moon’s gravitational pull also plays a major role in shaping ocean tides, making it one of the most influential celestial bodies in daily life on our planet.
