A view of Seattle across the Elliott Bay
Elliott Bay is a central inlet of Puget Sound on the western edge of Seattle, one of the largest cities in the Pacific Northwest of the United States. For generations, the bay has shaped the city’s identity as a maritime center, linking its shoreline to shipping, ferries, recreation, and trade. Today, the Seattle waterfront remains one of the city’s most recognizable public spaces, where urban life meets the waters of the inland sea.
The bay also opens onto a broader landscape of islands, inlets, and distant mountain views that help define the character of the region. Seattle grew rapidly in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and its relationship with Elliott Bay played a major role in that development. Seen from the shoreline, this stretch of water offers a clear reminder of how closely the city’s history, economy, and daily rhythms are tied to Puget Sound.
